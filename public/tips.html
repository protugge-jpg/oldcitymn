Discover the Heart of Mongolia

Practical travel guidance and local insights for an unforgettable journey.

Welcome to Mongolia

Whether you’re planning a short escape or a longer adventure, Mongolia welcomes you with open arms and warm hearts. Wherever your journey takes you, the local people will greet you with genuine hospitality and are always ready to offer a helping hand.

This land of endless horizons offers a stunning mix of untouched steppes, the mystical Gobi Desert, and the majestic Khangai mountains. Its raw, natural beauty captivates travelers from around the world.

One of Mongolia’s most enriching experiences is visiting a nomadic herder family. Here, you’ll witness a way of life that has remained unchanged for centuries—living in harmony with nature, raising livestock, and thriving in the vast, open landscapes.

Enhance your journey with unforgettable adventures such as horseback or camel riding across the countryside, and feel the true spirit of Mongolia through its people, culture, and endless skies.

Mongolia isn’t just a destination—it’s a journey to the soul.

Helpful Tips for Your Trip to Mongolia

  1. Travel SIM Card

    You can purchase a travel SIM card either at Chinggis Khaan International Airport or from designated locations in downtown Ulaanbaatar. It’s a convenient way to stay connected during your journey.

  2. Travel Expectations

    Tourism infrastructure in Mongolia is still developing, so some services might be limited or not at the level you are used to. We kindly suggest being prepared for this. That said, Mongolia offers many unique and rewarding experiences that more than make up for any minor inconveniences.

  3. Best Travel Season

    When planning your trip, we highly recommend choosing a suitable time of year. The best season for tourism is from mid-June to mid-October. During this period, it’s easier to travel solo, with friends, or by joining group tours. Outside of this season, tour companies tend to operate less frequently, and solo travel may become more expensive. However, if you’re traveling in a group, trips are possible year-round, and shared costs make it more budget-friendly.

  4. Make Reservations in Advance

    Always confirm bookings in advance—whether it’s accommodation, dining, or other services. Contacting the place beforehand will help avoid misunderstandings or unexpected issues, and ensure you select the right option. This can save you both time and money.

  5. Weather and Packing Smart

    Prepare your clothing and luggage according to the season and expected weather. Leave behind bulky or unnecessary items when possible. Most hotels and guesthouses offer storage services, which will help you travel lighter. Mongolia has an extreme climate: summer temperatures can reach +40°C and winter can drop to -40°C. In spring and autumn, it’s even possible to experience all four seasons in one day. Also, keep in mind about Mongolia’s elevation—it’s situated 1,580 meters above sea level.

  6. Traffic in Ulaanbaatar

    Due to high population density and vehicle congestion, Ulaanbaatar often experiences heavy traffic throughout the day. Plan your travel routes carefully and allow extra time. Choosing the right mode of transportation will help ensure you reach your destinations on time.

Getting Around in Ulaanbaatar

Taxi Services

You can use the UBCab mobile app to request a taxi to your location. This app-based service is convenient but may include extra charges for dispatch and waiting time.

Alternatively, you can hail a car on the street by raising your hand. In Mongolia, it’s common for private cars to act as taxis—even if they don’t have a taxi sign. In such cases, it’s important to negotiate the fare with the driver in advance. The average fare for regular taxis is about 2,500 MNT per kilometer.

Public Transportation

If you prefer public buses, the fare is 1,000 MNT per ride. However, depending on your destination, you may need to transfer once or multiple times.

Travel Options and Local Transportation Tips

  • Plan Your Route & Travel Mode Wisely: Whether you’re traveling solo or in a group, we recommend carefully planning your travel route and choosing the right mode of transport. In rural areas, public transportation may only take you part of the way—once there, you can continue your journey by arranging a ride with a local driver, or even explore on foot if you prefer a more immersive experience.
  • Renting a Vehicle with a Local Driver: Another option is to rent a private vehicle and driver directly from Ulaanbaatar, either for short or long-term trips. This is highly recommended, as Mongolia’s vast and rugged terrain can be difficult to navigate—GPS doesn’t always work in remote areas, and road conditions may lead to unexpected mechanical issues. Most local drivers have excellent knowledge of the countryside routes. While English-speaking drivers are rare, it’s not impossible to find one. Vehicles can also be rented with or without a driver, depending on your needs.
  • Off-the-Grid Adventures: In rural Mongolia, there’s often no mobile signal outside of towns and villages. You will frequently travel off-road through untouched landscapes. Many travelers enjoy camping in nature—you can set up tents anywhere for free, cook your own meals, and enjoy a peaceful night under the stars. It’s not only a unique and unforgettable experience, but also a budget-friendly option.
  • Join a Tour Group: If you prefer not to plan everything on your own, you can easily contact a tour company and join an existing travel group. This is a convenient and social way to explore Mongolia while everything is arranged for you.

More Practical Advice

  1. Be Mindful of Local Food and Eating Habits

    No matter where your adventure takes you, it’s important to be cautious with the food and drinks you consume, especially if you’re trying something new or unfamiliar. In Mongolian tradition, after eating a heavy or rich meal, locals sometimes drink a small amount (around 100 ml) of vodka to help with digestion and prevent stomach discomfort. While this practice is optional, some travelers find it helpful—just remember to drink responsibly and according to your own comfort and health needs.

  2. Currency Exchange

    If you decide to travel to Mongolia, it is better to bring your own local currency (though you should research this in advance). There are foreign exchange markets in Mongolia where you can exchange around 20 different foreign currencies. Many tourists bring US dollars or euros, but buying these foreign currencies in your country and then converting them into Mongolian tugriks upon arrival can lead to a notable cash loss due to exchange rate differences. Most travelers arrive in Mongolia with US dollars. However, please note that all payments in Mongolia must be made in the local currency, the Mongolian tugrik. In short, you will need to exchange your home currency into Mongolian tugriks when you arrive.
